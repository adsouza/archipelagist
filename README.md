# Archipelagist

A single-player isometric settlement game written in Zig with raylib-zig. Java starts with a harbor house, a cottage, two settlers, and enough timber to begin a second industry. Settle Bali, light its lighthouse, and bridge the islands to unlock construction of larger ships and explore farther waters.

## Play

Use Zig 0.17.1 and run `zig build run -Doptimize=ReleaseSafe` from this directory. The first build fetches the pinned raylib-zig dependency and its native graphics dependencies. To install the executable locally, run `zig build -Doptimize=ReleaseSafe`. Every build installs to `zig-out/<os>-<arch>/bin`, such as `zig-out/macos-arm64/bin`, and a native build also points `zig-out/bin` at its own folder (on macOS and Linux), so `zig-out/bin/archipelagist` is always the one that runs on this machine. To build for x86_64 Linux from macOS or any other host, run `zig build -Dtarget=x86_64-linux-gnu -Doptimize=ReleaseSafe`; it installs to `zig-out/linux-x86_64/bin` and leaves `zig-out/bin` alone. The Linux binary runs on any X11 desktop (or XWayland), which provides the X11 and OpenGL libraries the game loads at startup. The executable includes the fonts, map artwork, and sound effects. When starting a new charter, timber creaks and the anchor hits the water. Tool selection, construction, trade, and sailing play short cues. The harbor is silent while idle.

Java and Bali share a map with Sumatra, Borneo, Sulawesi, the islands east of Lombok, New Guinea and nearby Pacific islands, Singapore, and much of the Philippines. The regional map uses simplified outlines from [Natural Earth’s 1:10m land data](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-physical-vectors/10m-land/), rescaled for play. The settlement grids blend these outlines with the original game coastlines. Select **Regional map** to see the full chart. Scroll to zoom, drag or use the arrow keys to pan, and press **Home** to return to Java and Bali. Use the **Island Stores** dropdown or press **Tab** to switch the stockpile and trade view. Click an available island on the inset **Island map** or on the full regional chart to select its stores and jump to its port. Use **Hide** to collapse the inset and **Show island map** to reopen it. All 44 destinations on the regional chart can be settled. Yamdena represents Tanimbar, and Trangan represents Aru. All construction tiles use the same scale. Grid sizes follow the coastlines, so larger islands have more room to build. Survey voyages open coastal ports on arrival. Coins and total settlers appear under **Charter totals**.

## The island charter

Store counts pulse red when they are falling and drop below 12 timber, 6 stone, or three days of food or water for the island's residents.

- Select a construction card or press **1–8**, then click a land tile on an island with at least one resident. Woodlots need pine forest, and grain farms need open ground. Right-click to switch to inspection.
- Press **5** to lay roads for one stone per tile. Roads join along shared tile edges and link to neighboring buildings. Settlers can walk from the harbor through neighboring buildings, forest, and resource sites. Roads connect these features across open gaps. Each reachable tree with orange fruit supplies three fruit per day at full strength.
- Press **2** to build a woodlot, **6** to quarry a rocky outcrop, or **7** to build a hunting lodge where deer graze. Woodlots and lodges can harvest up to three units a day from a local stock of twelve. Once depleted, a woodlot takes three game days to regrow and a deer herd takes two to recover. Right-click to deselect your tool, then click a woodlot or lodge to check its stock and recovery time. Quarries yield three stone per day. Fresh and upgraded ledgers start with eight stone.
- Press **8** to build a well on open ground inland for five timber and two stone. Each well can supply up to four fresh water per day. The ledger shows the island’s daily water cap. Once the wells reach that cap, building more wells adds no water output. The ship brings twenty fresh water at the start. That reserve is unloaded at Java. Each island needs its own water supply.
- Every seven seconds, each established harbor supplies one timber and one grain. Each woodlot adds three timber and each farm adds five grain per day. Each day, settlers consume one unit each of grain, venison, fruit, and water per pair, rounded up. A grain shortage halts staffed production until another day of grain rations. Each day short of venison reduces strength, and six days of accumulated deficit stop work. Adequate meat reduces the deficit by one per day. Starting on the seventh consecutive day short of fruit, scurvy claims a settler per day until fruit rations resume. A water shortage can also cost a settler. A fed island with at least three food left, two residents, and a free bed can grow by one settler. When several islands qualify, they take turns. Reserved beds stay available for passengers until they arrive.
- Each cottage houses four people. When the settlements have enough supplies, the treasury collects one coin per four settlers each day. Export six grain from the selected island for ten coins using the harbor ledger.
- Open **Shipyard** and choose **Harbor ferry**. It lists the harbors the ferry can reach from where it is moored. Survey Bali for twenty coins. A foghorn marks the ferry’s arrival at Bali. The dock opens and the ferry stays there until you choose **Sail empty** to Java. Once it docks at Java, choose **Load and board** to send settlers and supplies. The ferry carries one unit of grain or venison per pair of settlers for each day at sea, so two for the Bali Strait. A new camp can receive up to four settlers before you build cottages.
- The ferry sails any short lane between harbors: a crossing no longer than the Bali Strait that passes no other island. From Bali it reaches Lombok, Madura, and Borneo, and from Lombok it reaches Sumbawa and beyond. After the charter is complete it can survey these harbors itself for twenty coins.
- With eight settlers, build the lighthouse on Bali for twenty-four local timber and thirty coins.
- Choose **Plan** by **Bridge the islands** in the charter. The two-span crossing of the Bali Strait needs four timber, six stone, and four resident settlers on each island. Once it opens, use **Cross bridge** to move settlers or transfer supplies overland. Bridges can reach up to two spans. Bali and Lombok are three spans apart, so reaching Lombok takes the ferry or a ship.

The ledger shows the next three unfinished charter goals. Later goals include settling Sulawesi, Luzon, and New Guinea and building a fleet of four survey brigs or ocean barques in any combination. Completing the Bali landing, lighthouse, and bridge unlocks ship construction. Select an island in **Island Stores**, then open **Shipyard → Build ships**. Build survey brigs or, after charting Lombok, ocean barques. Construction uses the island’s timber and stone and the shared treasury. Each dock has two berths shared by the ferry and expedition ships. A departing ship reserves a berth at its destination until it docks. Ships built away from Java can sail there using local provisions.

| Vessel | Hold | Range |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Harbor ferry | 32 units | Harbors one short lane apart |
| Survey brig | 120 units | Lombok, Borneo, Bangka, Belitung, Sumbawa, Flores, Sumba, Madura, Siberut, and Buton |
| Ocean barque | 288 units | All expedition destinations, including New Guinea |

The hold carries voyage rations and cargo together. Expedition crews take grain, venison, fruit, and water for the round trip. Settlers take rations for the outward leg. Longer crossings take more of the hold.

In **Expedition ships**, use **Previous ship** and **Next ship** to choose a vessel. Each ship can sail independently. Browse destinations with **Previous**, **Next**, or the mouse wheel. Survey a destination, then send the ship back to Java before choosing **Load and board**. You can send settlers, supplies, or both. First settlement voyages start with camp supplies based on available stock and space. Drag the passenger and cargo sliders to adjust the manifest. The focused slider also responds to the arrow keys. Use **Launch voyage** when ready. The ship unloads and stays moored at the destination. Choose **Return to Java** in the Shipyard to send it home using its reserved provisions. The islands support local construction, production, and upkeep. Use **View port** to zoom to the selected settlement and **Watch voyage** in the Shipyard to follow a ship at sea.

**Space** pauses or resumes. Click the speed control to cycle through normal speed, triple speed, and pause. Progress is saved automatically during play and when you close the game. Launching again resumes the saved charter and restores the window’s last position and size. **F5** saves immediately and **F9** reloads the latest save. Use **Restart** and confirm **Start over** to replace it with a new charter.

The charter ends when the last settler dies. The game saves the final state and offers **Start over** or **Quit**.

Archipelagist saves to `archipelagist.sav` in `~/Library/Application Support/Archipelagist` on macOS and in `$XDG_DATA_HOME/Archipelagist` or `~/.local/share/Archipelagist` on Linux. The game reads only saves from its current version. If a save can’t be read, the game keeps the file and offers **Start over**.

Original map, buildings, ship, and vegetation are drawn with raylib primitives. Atkinson Hyperlegible and DM Serif Display are bundled under the SIL Open Font License in `src/app/fonts/`. The sound effects in `src/app/sfx/` were generated with GPT for this game.

## Development

Run the tests with `zig build test -Doptimize=ReleaseSafe`. It first runs `zig build check-deps`, which checks that every `@import` points down the layers described in ARCHITECTURE.md. GitHub Actions runs the tests and builds the game on Linux and macOS for every push to `main` and every pull request, and checks the x86_64 Linux cross-build from macOS. To run the dependency check before each commit, run `git config core.hooksPath tools/git-hooks` once in your clone.

See [Architecture and diagnostics](ARCHITECTURE.md) for command and query boundaries, local traces, the command journal and headless replay (`zig build replay -- JOURNAL`), and the property and mutation test commands.
