# Archipelagist

A single-player isometric settlement game written in Zig with raylib-zig. Java starts with a harbor house, a cottage, two settlers, and enough timber to begin a second industry. Settle Bali, light its lighthouse, and bridge the islands to unlock construction of larger ships and explore farther waters.

## Play

Use Zig 0.17.1 and run `zig build run -Doptimize=ReleaseSafe` from this directory. The first build fetches the pinned raylib-zig dependency and its native graphics dependencies. To install the executable locally, run `zig build -Doptimize=ReleaseSafe`. Every build installs to `zig-out/<os>-<arch>/bin`, such as `zig-out/macos-arm64/bin`, and a native build also points `zig-out/bin` at its own folder (on macOS and Linux), so `zig-out/bin/archipelagist` is always the one that runs on this machine. To build for x86_64 Linux from macOS or any other host, run `zig build -Dtarget=x86_64-linux-gnu -Doptimize=ReleaseSafe`; it installs to `zig-out/linux-x86_64/bin` and leaves `zig-out/bin` alone. The Linux binary runs on any X11 desktop (or XWayland), which provides the X11 and OpenGL libraries the game loads at startup. To build the browser version, run `zig build -Dtarget=wasm32-emscripten -Doptimize=ReleaseSmall`. It installs a static site to `zig-out/web`; the first web build downloads the Emscripten SDK. Run `zig build run -Dtarget=wasm32-emscripten` to serve it locally and open it in a browser. Every push to `main` publishes it to GitHub Pages at https://adsouza.github.io/archipelagist/. The executable includes the fonts, map artwork, and sound effects. When starting a new charter, timber creaks and the anchor hits the water. Tool selection, construction, trade, and sailing play short cues. The harbor is silent while idle.

Java and Bali share a map with Sumatra, Borneo, Sulawesi, the islands east of Lombok, New Guinea and nearby Pacific islands, Singapore, and much of the Philippines. The regional map uses simplified outlines from [Natural Earth’s 1:10m land data](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-physical-vectors/10m-land/), rescaled for play. The settlement grids blend these outlines with the original game coastlines. Select **Regional map** to see the full chart. Scroll to zoom and drag or use the arrow keys to pan. Press **Q** or **E**, or use the turn buttons, to rotate the camera around the center of the map. **Home** restores the opening angle and returns to Java and Bali. Hover over a tile to see through any ship or bridge that covers it. Use the **Island Stores** dropdown or press **Tab** to switch the stockpile and trade view. Click an available island on the inset **Island map** or on the full regional chart to select its stores and jump to its port. Use **Hide** to collapse the inset and **Show island map** to reopen it. All 44 destinations on the regional chart can be settled. Yamdena represents Tanimbar, and Trangan represents Aru. All construction tiles use the same scale. Grid sizes follow the coastlines, so larger islands have more room to build. Survey voyages open coastal ports on arrival. Coins and total settlers appear under **Charter totals**.

## The island charter

Store counts pulse red when they are falling and drop below 12 timber, 6 stone, or three days of food or water for the island's residents.

- Select a construction card or press **1–9** or **0**, then click a tile on an island with at least one resident. Woodlots need pine forest, and grain farms need open ground. Right-click to switch to inspection.
- Press **5** to lay roads for one stone per tile. Roads join along shared tile edges and link to neighboring buildings. Settlers can walk from the harbor through neighboring buildings, forest, and resource sites. Roads connect these features across open gaps. Each reachable tree with orange fruit supplies three fruit per day at full strength.
- Press **2** to build a woodlot, **6** to quarry a rocky outcrop, or **7** to build a hunting lodge where deer graze. Woodlots and lodges can harvest up to three units a day from a local stock of twelve. Once depleted, a woodlot takes three game days to regrow and a deer herd takes two to recover. Right-click to deselect your tool, then click a woodlot or lodge to check its stock and recovery time. Quarries, woodlots and hunting lodges only work once settlers can reach them from the harbor, so lay roads to them; clicking an idle one says so. Quarries yield three stone per day. Every island has at least two rocky outcrops, two deer herds and two fruit trees that its harbor can reach with six road tiles or fewer. On Java, four outcrops, three herds and two trees are within three road tiles of the starting roads. Fresh and upgraded ledgers start with eight stone.
- Press **8** to build a well on open ground inland for five timber and two stone. Each well can supply up to four fresh water per day. The ledger shows the island’s daily water cap. Once the wells reach that cap, building more wells adds no water output. The ship brings twenty fresh water at the start. That reserve is unloaded at Java. Each island needs its own water supply.
- Every seven seconds, each established harbor supplies one timber and one grain. Each woodlot adds three timber and each farm adds five grain per day. Each day, settlers consume one unit each of grain, venison, fruit, and water per pair, rounded up. A grain shortage halts staffed production until another day of grain rations. Each day short of venison reduces strength, and six days of accumulated deficit stop work. Adequate meat reduces the deficit by one per day. Starting on the seventh consecutive day short of fruit, scurvy claims a settler per day until fruit rations resume. A water shortage can also cost a settler. A fed island with at least three food left, two residents, and a free bed can grow by one settler. When several islands qualify, they take turns. Reserved beds stay available for passengers until they arrive.
- Each cottage houses four people. When the settlements have enough supplies, the treasury collects one coin per four settlers each day. Export six grain from the selected island for ten coins using the harbor ledger.
- Select **Town square** (**0**) and click the top tile of a clear 2×2 block to build a plaza for four timber and sixteen stone. Settlers can walk through plazas. The first square lets you name the town with up to 30 characters. Right-click to switch to inspection, then click any town square to name or rename its town. A town square unlocks apartment construction on its island. Select **Apartments** (**9**) and click a cottage to replace it with a twelve-bed apartment building for twelve timber and eight stone.
- Open **Shipyard** and choose **Harbor ferry**. It lists the harbors the ferry can sail to or survey from its current berth, including only destinations with room to dock. Surveys also require twenty coins and the relevant charter progress. Choose **Survey** by Bali to load the survey for twenty coins. Up to four settlers and their camp supplies can sail with the survey crew and go ashore as soon as Bali is charted, or the crew can sail alone. A foghorn marks the ferry’s arrival at Bali. The dock opens and the ferry stays there until you choose **Sail empty** to Java. From a charted harbor, choose **Load and board** to send settlers and supplies. The ferry carries one unit of grain or venison per pair of settlers for each day at sea, so two for the Bali Strait. A new camp can receive up to four settlers before you build cottages.
- The ferry sails any short lane between harbors: a crossing no longer than the Bali Strait that passes no other island. From Bali it reaches Lombok, Madura, and Borneo, and from Lombok it reaches Sumbawa and beyond. After the charter is complete it can survey these harbors itself for twenty coins.
- Press **4** to build a lighthouse on any settled island for twenty-four local timber and thirty coins once the charter has eight settlers. Choose a land tile that shares an edge with water and is within four edge-to-edge tile steps of the local harbor. Each island can build one lighthouse. Bali’s lighthouse also counts toward the opening charter.
- Choose **Plan** by **Bridge the islands** in the charter. The two-span crossing of the Bali Strait needs four timber, six stone, and four resident settlers on each island. Once it opens, lay a road from each harbor to its end of the bridge: the bridge screen shows how many road tiles each side still needs and offers **Lay road** (one stone per tile; Java's side needs one tile, Bali's about 23). Then the two islands are joined: they share one store, so either island can build, eat and ship from the same goods, and the stores panel says which island it shares with. Settlers walk over by themselves, one per bridge per day, to an island with fewer than two settlers or from an island whose beds are full to one with beds to spare. A walk takes three seconds of game time per span and holds a bed on the far side. Quarries, woodlots, lodges and fruit trees on the far island count as connected through the bridge. Bridges can reach up to two spans. Bali and Lombok are three spans apart, so reaching Lombok takes the ferry or a ship. Madura is one span from Java's north coast: once Madura is charted and settled, **Bridges → Next bridge** plans that crossing for two timber, three stone, and four resident settlers on each island.

The ledger shows the next three unfinished charter goals. Later goals include settling Sulawesi, Luzon, and New Guinea and building a fleet of four survey brigs or ocean barques in any combination. Completing the Bali landing, lighthouse, and bridge unlocks ship construction. Select an island in **Island Stores**, then open **Shipyard → Build ships**. Build survey brigs or, after charting Lombok, ocean barques. Construction uses the island’s timber and stone and the shared treasury. Each harbor starts with one berth shared by the ferry and expedition ships. A lighthouse opens a second berth. Departing ships reserve their berths at their destinations. Ships already docked or booked in an older save keep their berths.

| Vessel | Hold | Range |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Harbor ferry | 32 units | Harbors one short lane apart |
| Survey brig | 120 units | Shorter passages, including Bali to Siberut or Buton |
| Ocean barque | 288 units | Longer passages, including Java to New Guinea |

An expedition ship keeps its crew aboard when it docks. It can sail onward from any open harbor, including uninhabited islands, using supplies already aboard and topping up from the port as needed. Reachable destinations depend on the departure harbor and hull. The hold carries voyage rations and cargo together. Expedition crews carry grain, venison, fruit, and water. Ships already carrying provisions can sail one-way if they have enough for the outward leg and cannot provision both legs. Unused provisions stay aboard. Settlers take rations for the outward leg. Longer crossings take more of the hold.

In **Expedition ships**, use **Previous ship** and **Next ship** to choose a vessel. The map marks destinations the ship can sail to now, based on its current harbor, range, supplies, and available berths. Hover over an island for the crossing time and harbor status, then click to open the manifest. Right-click a charted destination to visit its port. Scroll or use **+** and **−** to zoom, drag to pan, and select **Fit map** to see the full region. On a survey, up to four settlers and their supplies can land as soon as the harbor is charted, or the crew can sail alone. At a charted port you can send settlers and supplies or choose **Sail empty**. First settlement voyages start with camp supplies based on available stock and space. Drag the passenger and cargo sliders to adjust the manifest. The focused slider also responds to the arrow keys. Use **Launch survey** or **Launch voyage** when ready. The ship unloads and stays moored at the destination. A ship can use its remaining provisions to return or continue onward. The manifest shows what is already aboard and what comes from the departure port. If a harbor is available only by returning, the map marks it **Return only**. You can also use the Shipyard’s **Return to** button. Use **View port** to zoom to the ship’s current harbor and **Watch voyage** to follow a ship at sea.

**Space** pauses or resumes. Click the speed control to cycle through normal speed, triple speed, and pause. Progress is saved automatically during play and when you close the game. Launching again resumes the saved charter and restores the window’s last position and size. **F5** saves immediately and **F9** reloads the latest save. Use **Restart** and confirm **Start over** to replace it with a new charter.

The charter ends when the last settler dies. The game saves the final state and offers **Start over** or **Quit**.

Archipelagist saves to `archipelagist.sav` in `~/Library/Application Support/Archipelagist` on macOS and in `$XDG_DATA_HOME/Archipelagist` or `~/.local/share/Archipelagist` on Linux. In the browser it saves to the site's IndexedDB storage, so clearing site data deletes the save. Saves made before town naming load with their towns unnamed. If a save can’t be read, the game keeps the file and offers **Start over**.

Original map, buildings, ship, and vegetation are drawn with raylib primitives. Atkinson Hyperlegible, DM Serif Display, and [GNU Unifont](https://www.unifoundry.com/unifont/) are bundled under the SIL Open Font License in `src/app/fonts/`. The sound effects in `src/app/sfx/` were generated with GPT for this game.

## Development

Run the tests with `zig build test -Doptimize=ReleaseSafe`. It first runs `zig build check-deps`, which checks that every `@import` points down the layers described in ARCHITECTURE.md. GitHub Actions runs the tests and builds the game on Linux and macOS for every push to `main` and every pull request, checks the x86_64 Linux cross-build from macOS, and publishes the browser build to GitHub Pages. To run the dependency check before each commit, run `git config core.hooksPath tools/git-hooks` once in your clone.

See [Architecture and diagnostics](ARCHITECTURE.md) for command and query boundaries, local traces, the command journal and headless replay (`zig build replay -- JOURNAL`), and the property and mutation test commands.
